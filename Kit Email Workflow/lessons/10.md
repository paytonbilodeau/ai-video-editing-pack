# Lesson 10

Subject: Give your AI a better editing education

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

Better references helped my AI editor. I gave it tutorials, transcripts, documentation and examples from people actually doing the work.

Two of the posts I used were [Alex's editing process](https://x.com/The_Alex/status/2097847043796725924) and [Future Brian's walkthrough](https://x.com/ForwardEditor/status/2097126303765864768). Alex describes reviewing the edit and giving timestamped feedback; Brian describes repeated revisions and crashes. I find those working details more useful than a perfect-looking demo with the hard parts removed.

Learning a little editing vocabulary helps too. A J-cut starts the next shot's audio before its picture appears. Once you know the name, you can ask the AI to try a J-cut at a specific transition and compare it with a straight cut.

Illustration: Turn a tutorial into one tested technique, and save the part that worked.

Choose one recent tutorial for your editor and the thing you want to improve. If the AI can't retrieve the video or transcript, upload the relevant material or paste the useful section.

Ask your AI:

Extract the techniques that apply to this edit. Check version-specific steps against current official documentation. Explain one technique in plain language, then test it on a short section of my footage. For timing, movement or color, inspect the visual reference as well as the transcript. Show me the result, and save the useful instruction after I accept it.

Giving the AI references and saving instructions doesn't retrain the underlying model. It gives the next editing session better information to work from.

Try one technique today. If it makes the edit better, keep it. You don't have to adopt every trick in the tutorial.
