# Lesson 9

Subject: How I got AI to understand the look I wanted

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

When I made [the Netflix editing-style video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y-qpMzjmSBU), I didn't start with “make it look cool.” I collected reference images, described the movement and sound, and tested a small piece of footage. Then I kept changing the result until it felt right for my videos.

Choose one effect first. You might want a label that follows an object, a title that appears handwritten, or a diagram that builds as you explain it. Pick the effect that makes your point easier to understand.

Illustration: Translate a reference into one specific visual behavior and test it on your own footage.

Put a few clear examples in a reference folder and tell the AI what you like about each. For motion, include an accessible video and timestamps. A still image can show the design, but it can't show the entrance, hold or exit.

Use a brief like this:

Study these references for the specific effect I described. Explain the movement, timing, placement, typography and sound. Suggest an original version for my footage and brand, using my own words and assets. Build a short editable test over the actual shot. Show me the entrance, the time it stays on screen and the exit.

Be specific about the feel. “The label should follow the mug, settle gently and stay readable while I explain it” gives the AI a much better target than “add some cinematic motion.”

Watch the whole test with the dialogue. A beautiful animation on an empty background can still cover your face, block the demonstration or compete with the point.

The [motion guide](https://github.com/paytonbilodeau/ai-video-editing-pack/blob/main/AI%20Video%20Editing%20Pack/references/MOTION%20AND%20LUTS.md) walks through the brief. Today, choose one reference and one effect worth trying.
