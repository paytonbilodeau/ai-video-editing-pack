# Lesson 12

Subject: The graphic looks good. Can anyone read it?

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

An animation can look pretty sick and still make the video worse. It might arrive before the idea does, disappear while you're halfway through reading it, or put a giant arrow on something that didn't need an arrow.

Give every graphic a job. It should help someone notice, understand or remember the point you're making. If you can't name that job, try the video without the graphic.

Illustration: Leave a graphic on screen long enough to read it, and check it at phone size.

Then check the animation with the moving shot and sound together:

> Review this graphic over the moving shot with sound. Does it arrive with the idea, stay long enough to read and avoid my face and the demonstration? Does the sound effect mask any dialogue?
>
> Suggest the smallest useful change. Render that section so I can compare the timing, picture and sound with the previous version.

Read the words at a normal pace. Check them on your phone, and watch the entrance and exit as well as the nicest frame in the middle. A smaller screen reveals tiny type and crowded layouts quickly.

If it feels busy, remove something. A clear label may be enough without the bounce, glow, whoosh and three supporting arrows. I'd rather the viewer understand the idea than notice how much animation I can now generate.

Compare the revised section with the previous version. Keep the one that helps the explanation.
