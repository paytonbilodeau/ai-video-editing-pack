# Lesson 8

Subject: Yes, AI can help you make a LUT

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

If the corrected image already looks good, you can leave it alone. Shocking, I know. If you want a specific look, a LUT is one useful way to explore it.

A LUT is a lookup table: a file that maps input colors to output colors. Your editor applies that mapping across the image. AI can help create a .cube LUT, which is a common format that video editors can import.

Start with a direction you can describe, such as warmer highlights, cooler shadows or softer contrast. Give the AI a reference and several frames from your own footage. A movie still shows the look you like; your frames show the camera, room and lighting that the LUT actually needs to work on.

Illustration: A creative LUT maps color globally, and should be tested on a separate clip after correction.

Ask your AI:

Help me create a downloadable .cube LUT inspired by this reference and suited to my source frames. Confirm the input and output color spaces first. Keep the technical conversion separate from the creative look. Preserve believable skin, neutral colors and highlight detail. Give me the actual LUT file, explain how to import it into my editor, and test it on a clip we haven't used to build the look.

Apply the creative LUT after the footage has been converted into the right working color space. Compare it on and off across several shots, including a new clip with different lighting.

A LUT changes colors globally. It can't recognize your face and brighten only that area, so keep masks and other local corrections separate.

Use [the full LUT testing method](https://github.com/paytonbilodeau/ai-video-editing-pack/blob/main/AI%20Video%20Editing%20Pack/references/MOTION%20AND%20LUTS.md) to check the result. Choose the look that suits your video, rather than the one that looks most dramatic on a single frame.
