# Lesson 19

Subject: Watch the file, not just your timeline

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

After looking at the same timeline for a while, your brain starts filling in what you meant to make. The exported file can still contain a clipped word, a missing graphic or a weird audio jump.

Watch the actual file you plan to upload from beginning to end, with sound, at normal speed. If you use a final audio service, check its finished output too. A timeline preview won't reveal a problem introduced during export or audio processing.

Illustration: Approve the actual exported video at normal speed, including its picture and sound.

Pay attention to the first and last seconds, the cut points, the dialogue level, and whether each caption stays long enough to read. For a vertical video, check it on your phone too.

I sometimes use faster playback to spot repetition or sections that drag, but I return to normal speed for the final check. Fast playback can hide a bad cut and make readable text disappear before you get a fair look at it.

Write down the timestamp and the specific problem as you watch. Then ask the AI to fix those moments in the editable project.

> Use these notes and timestamps from the exact file I reviewed. Fix the problems in the editable project while preserving approved sections.
>
> Render short previews of the changes. After approval, save the updated project and export a new version. If final audio processing is needed, use the new pre-service export once. Give me the finished file to check before posting.

Watch the new file before posting it. Check the changed sections closely and make sure the rest still plays correctly. It's very easy to fix the timeline and accidentally upload yesterday's export.

This is the last bit of taste and quality control that makes the AI's first pass something you're happy to publish.
