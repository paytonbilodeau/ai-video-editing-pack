# Lesson 4

Subject: Please stop cutting off the end of my words

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

A cut can look perfectly tidy on a timeline and sound awful when you play it. The end of a word disappears, the next sentence arrives too early, or a tiny awkward gap appears before every clip.

The cut point is where one clip ends and the next begins. Have your AI review a few seconds on both sides with the sound playing. A transcript helps locate the sentence; the recording tells you where the word actually ends.

Illustration: Place a cut between complete words, while protecting useful pauses.

Try this on your first cut:

Review the cut points in this section with the audio playing. Check for clipped syllables, repeated words, awkward gaps and changes in meaning. Keep natural breaths and pauses that help the explanation. Tighten dead space without rushing the speaker. Leave enough time to see each demonstration. If a cut clips a word, move the boundary until the complete word is audible, then play the transition again.

Don't ask it to remove every silence. A pause after a useful point gives someone time to think. A pause while I forget what I was saying is rather less useful.

Watch one minute and pick the worst cut. Tell the AI what you hear, ask for a revision, and compare the two versions at normal speed.
