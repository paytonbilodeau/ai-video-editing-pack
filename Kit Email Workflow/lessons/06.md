# Lesson 6

Subject: Your audio doesn't need to sound aggressively clean

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

There's a point where noise reduction starts removing bits of your voice along with the noise. Lovely. We've cleaned the recording so thoroughly that I now sound like I'm underwater.

Start by telling your AI which audio to use: camera audio, a wireless mic, a shotgun mic, a dynamic mic, or a separate recording. If you have multiple tracks, have it identify the main dialogue, check sync, and avoid playing two copies of the same voice together.

Then name what you can hear. A constant hiss needs a different fix from a low rumble, room echo, or sentences that keep jumping in volume.

Illustration: Clean the specific noise without damaging the natural voice.

Copy this into your AI:

Use the pack's audio skill to inspect the dialogue source and identify the main audible problem. Start with my editor's native tools and treat that problem lightly. Use EQ or compression only where it helps: EQ changes the balance of frequencies, and compression controls volume differences. Give me an untreated and treated sample at comparable listening volume, including quiet words and a cut. Keep the treatment editable so we can adjust it.

Compare the samples at a similar volume because louder often sounds better even when the processing isn't helping. Listen for clear speech, natural consonants and breaths that still sound like a person.

The [audio guide](https://github.com/paytonbilodeau/ai-video-editing-pack/blob/main/AI%20Video%20Editing%20Pack/references/AUDIO%20GUIDE.md) covers the different mic and recording situations. You don't need to stack three enhancement tools on top of each other to make dialogue sound professional.

Check it on headphones and an ordinary speaker. Keep the version that's clearer and still sounds like you.
