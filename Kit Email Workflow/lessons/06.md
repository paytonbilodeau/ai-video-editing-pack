# Lesson 6

Subject: Your audio doesn't need to sound aggressively clean

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

There's a point where noise reduction starts removing bits of your voice along with the noise. Lovely. We've cleaned the recording so thoroughly that I now sound like I'm underwater.

Start by telling your AI which audio to use: camera audio, a wireless mic, a shotgun mic, a dynamic mic, or a separate recording. If you have multiple tracks, have it identify the main dialogue, check sync, and avoid playing two copies of the same voice together.

Then name what you can hear. A constant hiss needs a different fix from a low rumble, room echo, or sentences that keep jumping in volume.

Illustration: Clean the specific noise without damaging the natural voice.

Copy this into your AI:

> Use the pack's audio skill to identify the dialogue source, microphone type and main audible problem. Check sync and avoid duplicate dialogue tracks. Repair that problem lightly with my editor's tools, keeping the settings editable.
>
> Give me untreated and treated samples at comparable listening volume, including quiet words and a cut. If a final audio service is planned, avoid adding a second full enhancement chain.

EQ changes the balance of frequencies, such as reducing a low rumble. Compression controls volume differences. Use either because the recording needs it, not because the settings panel looks impressively complicated.

Compare the samples at similar volume. Louder often sounds better even when the processing isn't helping. Listen for clear words, natural consonants and breaths that still sound like a person. [The audio guide](https://github.com/paytonbilodeau/ai-video-editing-pack/blob/main/AI%20Video%20Editing%20Pack/references/AUDIO%20GUIDE.md) covers different mic and recording situations.

If you use Auphonic for the final audio pass, choose a preset suited to your microphone. Finish the cuts, color, captions and graphics first, then process that export once. It's optional and may cost money. Check the upload and cost before using it, and avoid stacking another full enhancement pass on top.

Check it on headphones and an ordinary speaker. Keep the version that's clearer and still sounds like you.
