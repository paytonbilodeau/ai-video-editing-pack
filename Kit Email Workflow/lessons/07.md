# 07 · Fix the picture before making it cinematic

Reading time: about 2 min.

If your skin is orange in one shot and blue in the next, a dramatic color grade probably won't sort that out.

Start with correction. Set a sensible exposure, get the white balance right, and match neighboring shots so they look like they belong in the same video. Exposure controls brightness; white balance removes an unwanted color cast.

Tell the AI how you recorded the footage. Log usually looks flat before the correct transform; HDR represents a wider brightness range than standard video. A color space describes how the colors and brightness are encoded. Confirm the camera settings and intended output so the editor uses the right conversion before adding a look.

![Fix exposure and white balance, then match adjacent shots before adding a look.](../assets/lesson-07.jpg)

Have the AI check the color scopes too. Scopes measure brightness and color, which helps catch lost highlight detail or a color cast that your display hides. [The guide explains the checks](../../AI%20Video%20Editing%20Pack/references/MOTION%20AND%20LUTS.md).

Let it make a light correction when the picture needs one. It should not apply the same brightening or color preset to every recording. A well-exposed shot may already be right.

Your AI should check the correction across the different lighting conditions in the recording, keeping the adjustments editable. A before-and-after preview is useful if you’re unsure about the look, but it doesn’t need to interrupt every edit. Tomorrow’s email looks at adding personality once the picture is right.
