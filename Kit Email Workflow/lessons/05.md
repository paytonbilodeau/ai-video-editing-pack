# Lesson 5

Subject: The boring first pass can be automatic

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

Sometimes I don't need the AI to make creative decisions yet. I want it to remove obvious dead space so I can start from a cleaner recording.

The pack includes an [optional local pre-edit route](https://github.com/paytonbilodeau/ai-video-editing-pack/blob/main/AI%20Video%20Editing%20Pack/skills/pre-edit/SKILL.md) for that. It points to the included free pre-edit tool, which runs on your computer. This route needs Python and FFmpeg, the software that processes the video. Some detection options also need a local speech model. Have your AI check the requirements and ask before installing anything.

Illustration: Review a proposed pre-edit plan on a copied clip before generating a first pass.

Ask your AI:

> Check whether the pack's local pre-edit route fits this recording and computer. Explain any missing software, download or footage limitation before setup. Show me a dry plan: the exact source, proposed removal ranges, reasons, detection method and output location.
>
> Explain what the method might miss. Keep the original intact, work from a copy and wait for approval before rendering.

A dry plan shows what the tool intends to do without applying the edit. Look at the proposed removals before you approve them. A quiet word, a thoughtful pause and a genuine mistake can all look like low-volume audio, so aggressive silence removal can damage an otherwise good recording.

The result is a flattened MP4: one finished video file, rather than a timeline of separate cuts. You can import it into an editor, but you can't simply drag each removed section back into place. Keep the source and removal plan so you can render a different first pass.

If your editor workflow already produces a good rough cut, skip this step. I'd rather get the video finished than add another tool because it exists.
