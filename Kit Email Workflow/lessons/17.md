# Lesson 17

Subject: Your vertical crop can hide the whole point

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

Your video can look great on a laptop and lose its whole point in a vertical crop. Your face is centered, but the button you're explaining is somewhere outside the frame. Excellent tutorial.

Automatic reframing gives you a starting point. You still need to check what the viewer has to see: the subject, the captions and the part of the demonstration that makes the explanation useful.

Illustration: Keep the important face and demonstration clear of mobile app controls.

Choose one platform and duplicate your approved timeline for its vertical version. Ask the AI to make a 10- to 15-second sample from a section with both speech and a screen demonstration.

> Create a 10- to 15-second vertical sample for my chosen platform on a duplicate timeline. Keep the important screen action visible and captions readable on a phone. Check current platform guidance and its preview controls before placing text.
>
> If the source doesn't fit, propose a layout that preserves the demonstration. Don't stretch the picture or crop away the part being explained.

Open the sample on your phone. Then use the platform's preview, if available, to see whether its buttons and caption area cover anything important. A safe area is the part of the frame where your essential content stays visible around those controls.

Sometimes a face above a screen recording works better than a tight crop. Sometimes you need to enlarge one part of the demonstration. Choose the layout that helps someone follow the video.

Fix that short sample before you export the rest. Ten seconds can tell you that the captions are too small. You don't need ten finished clips to confirm it.
