# Lesson 13

Subject: “Make it better” is a terrible revision note

Delay: 1 day after the previous lesson

The first attempt often needs specific corrections: movement that feels too fast, a sound that distracts, a caption shadow that's too heavy or a graphic that obscures the demonstration. Those details are the work.

When something feels wrong, name what you saw or heard and where it happened. “The graphic is bad” gives the AI very little to act on.

Illustration: Give a specific revision at a timecode and preserve the rest of the edit.

A useful note sounds like this: “At 00:24, the label covers the button I'm demonstrating. Move it above the button and keep it visible until the click.”

For pacing, try: “At 01:08, the pause before the next sentence drags. Shorten the dead space, but keep the breath and the complete first word.”

Use timestamps from the exact version you’re reviewing. For an uncertain change, a short preview can save a full export. For a clear correction, the AI can make it, check it and return the updated video.

> Use these timestamps from the version I reviewed. Apply the specific changes, preserve the rest of the edit and keep the previous version recoverable. Check each changed section with a few seconds before and after it, then deliver the updated project and export. Save any clear reusable preference in my editing profile; ask if a change might be a one-video exception.

If you're faster talking than typing, dictate your notes while watching. Say the timestamp, the problem and the change you want. I find that much more natural than trying to turn every thought into a perfect prompt.

You can ask for a preview whenever you want one. Otherwise, there’s no reason to approve the same straightforward correction twice.
