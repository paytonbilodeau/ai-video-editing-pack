# Comparisons, split production and specialized handoffs

## Keep comparison evidence fair

Choose the approved cut by source map and review state, not filename recency. Before selecting a derivative, inspect the actual picture for the introduction, proof and full concluding thought. A compelling sentence with only a tiny baked webcam may not support a full-camera verdict. Use another authentic complete take or an appropriate voiceover treatment, never unrelated mouth movement.

For paired demos, map each source independently to shared spoken/action/result anchors. Keep native timing; do not force equal duration by speeding one result. Record intentional holds and repetitions. Match useful visual scale and preserve every action/result needed for the claim. Play one intelligible audio source at a time unless a deliberate comparison needs otherwise, with original/loud/quiet/muted states preserved. If chapters were removed, call the selection retained chapters, not complete uncut demonstrations.

When retained footage visibly lags, keep necessary speech/evidence and explain only a known cause. If a notice is requested, use a readable message and actual spoken captions, clear the face/action, and remove dim/notice/captions together before the next meaningful demo. Do not show a fixed wait duration that no longer matches the cut. Detect freezes/duplicate-frame bursts as candidates for inspection, not automatic permission to discard speech.

For a narrow layout repair, transform the accepted group and preserve its relative geometry, cuts, captions and audio. If scaling exposes transparency, fill only the exposed backing rather than doubling a translucent background. Review phone-size margins and both boundaries. Native-resolution apertures and a once-computed static plate can avoid repeated resampling seams; keep editable layers.

## Split production only when useful

Track each part's source hash/range, exact output frames, rational FPS, settings/reference version, owned job, output, state and checks. Core intervals stay disjoint; context handles are removed exactly once. Translate half-open boundaries to the adapter's real convention. Inspect complete parts and every joined mouth/hand/eye, speech, exposure and motion transition. Good stills do not establish temporal continuity.

Retry failed parts and affected dependencies only. Inspect destination/job state after an uncertain result before resubmitting. A changed cut invalidates downstream timing; a changed style may invalidate its dependent visual parts. Preserve the last good version. After repeated failure, inspect the source/native state and change the method rather than rerunning the same recipe. Do not hide unexplained duration differences with a shortest-stream mux or time-stretched speech.

## CapCut finishing and separate stems

For the clean-master caption route, preserve an unobstructed caption region throughout. On a comparable 1080×1920 close portrait, a lower-chest band around y1320–1380 is a starting reference, then fit actual gestures, face, text and player controls. Distinct explanatory graphics may remain while spoken captions are left for CapCut. Optional SRT/word data must not cause a preview player to misrepresent the clean master as a burned-caption export.

An explicitly chosen stem handoff can provide a narration-plus-graphics master and separate full-length music/SFX stems beginning at zero. The creator can then perform final caption/enhancement/assembly. This is a specialized route; it does not silently replace the ordinary single final-mix audio order. Confirm microphone, caption owner, mix owner and intended external finish. A mixed audition is a review file, not proof of final assembly.

For suitable original high-frame-rate camera footage, preserve its exact source rate through grading and handoff. Verify any chosen enhancement in the actual installed CapCut build and inspect final motion/text. No borrowed app account, caption favorite, proprietary plugin or transform is included. Obtain licensed assets separately and verify their input/output contract.
