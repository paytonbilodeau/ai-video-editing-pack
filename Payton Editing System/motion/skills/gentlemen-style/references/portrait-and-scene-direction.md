## Generation workflow

Use native subscription generation. Supply real the customer photos as references whenever a person is being introduced or recreated. For an edit, identify each reference's role explicitly: the old image controls scene and text only; KIT controls identity and hair; the angle photo controls pose geometry. The old generic man's face must be replaced completely. Do not ask for a blend.

Start with a natural perspective that makes his face readable. A mild three-quarter view often gives more useful likeness evidence than the back of a head or a tiny full-body figure. Keep eyes, nose and mouth clear of hands, text and deep shadow. If a stronger angle is needed for the story, find a suitable real reference rather than forcing a frontal crop into it.

Build the complete still, including all final words and annotations, in the native generation. Keep text short and spell required words exactly. For a small correction to an accepted image, use a targeted native edit; preserve the face explicitly. Do not paste, warp or paint a face into the output with a separate compositing tool. Reference-photo crops and inspection thumbnails are allowed; they are not delivered creative edits.

Pose estimates help select references, not certify identity. The current local catalog gives coarse yaw/roll; **pitch was unavailable** and must not be invented. Landmark locations depend on pose, perspective and expression. Never promise exact biometrics, a perfect face match, a numerical likeness score or a persistent trained model that does not exist.
