# Visual DNA

Create a clean, slightly absurd explanation sketched on white space. It should feel drawn by a thoughtful product builder, not laid out as a course slide.

- Default to 16:9 landscape and pure white. No cream, grey, paper grain, gradients, shadows, or textured background.
- Use thin black pen lines with slight natural wobble, never polished vectors or heavy outlines.
- Let the subject occupy roughly 40 to 60 percent of the canvas, with at least 35 percent blank white space and a quiet region.
- Explain one action, structure, state, or metaphor per image.
- Use three to five short handwritten English labels when possible, no more than eight. Aim for one to four words per label. Follow an explicitly requested language instead.
- Let the picture show the structure. Do not write its category in a corner.

Color has a job: black for objects, characters, and main linework; orange for the main movement or path; red for a problem, warning, important point, or result; blue for secondary notes, feedback, or system state. Every color need not appear.

Avoid commercial illustration, cute mascots, children's cartoons, formal diagrams, dense architecture, tidy slide grids, realistic app screenshots, and long explanations embedded in the image. Keep it strange, clear, and restrained.
