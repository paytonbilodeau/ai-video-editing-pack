# 11 · Prompt your motion graphics

[All 22 lessons](<../README.md>) · [Open the plugin README](<../../README.md>)

Reading time: about 3 min.

You can describe a motion graphic and have AI build it without writing any code yourself. Then you can refine it in normal language, just as you would the rest of the edit.

Motion graphics are optional. If your footage and explanation already make the point, keep working toward the finished export.

Start with what the viewer needs to understand. “Show these three steps as I explain them” gives the AI a useful job and a reason for each part of the animation.

![Keep the editable motion-graphics source, render a video clip, then place it in your editor.](<assets/lesson-11.jpg>)

For a simple title or arrow, your video editor may already have everything needed. A keyframe records a setting at one moment. Give a title one position at the start and another a second later, and the editor creates the movement between them.

More involved graphics can use [Remotion](<https://www.remotion.dev/docs>), [HyperFrames](<https://hyperframes.heygen.com/quickstart>), or the included [JavaScript animation kit](<../../skills/javascript-animation/runtime/GUIDE.md>). These let the AI build animations with code. JavaScript Canvas acts like a drawing surface; Remotion can also include that animation in a larger project. You describe the result, and the AI handles the code.

The included [motion setup guide](<../../skills/style/references/MOTION TOOL SETUP.md>) helps the AI choose one suitable tool and test it with the supplied editable examples. Flow is the Remotion component toolkit; Remotion, HyperFrames and JavaScript are rendering tools. The visual style is a separate choice. Keep an engine that already works, and install another only when the scene needs it.

Have it test the animation over the actual footage, with the dialogue playing. Check the entrance, the time it stays visible, and the exit. If it sits over your video, ask for a transparent background in a format your editor supports; an ordinary MP4 won't provide transparency.

Once the movement works, add sound only if it helps. A click should line up with the visible click or landing, so trim any silence at the beginning of the sound file or move it earlier. Watch the exported result with sound to check the timing.

Keep the editable source, fonts and assets together. That way, changing three steps to four won't mean rebuilding the graphic from scratch.

### Check the animation

For an unfamiliar look, you can review storyboard frames before rendering. Then check the whole animation over the real footage: entrance, readable hold and exit. Keep the source editable and confirm that the preview and export agree.

For the included Tech Motion, Gentlemen or layered Sketch look, use the [matching source and render guide](<../../skills/remotion/flow/README.md>). Reproduce that example first, then change one detail. Neutral engine starters are separate checks.

The optional [Narrative Motion example](<../../skills/remotion/examples/narrative-motion/README.md>) shows another approach: keep one useful object on screen and change it as the idea develops. The included editable example uses invented timing. For your video, align it to the actual words and keep the useful content visible while the object moves.

### Put this into practice

Keep the motion source project as well as its render.

### Time the sound to the action

If a card lands at 2 seconds and the sound has 0.2 seconds of leading silence, start the untrimmed sound at 1.8 seconds or trim the silence. Keep the cue editable and check the export with sound.

Have the AI record the event frame, frame rate, audible onset in the sound file, source trim and clip start. Check frames before, at and after contact, plus the hold and exit. Listen to decide whether the cue fits the dialogue.
