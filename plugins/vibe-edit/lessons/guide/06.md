# 06 · Your audio doesn't need to sound aggressively clean

Reading time: about 2 min.

Noise reduction can start removing bits of your voice along with the noise, which isn't ideal when the result sounds like you're underwater.

Start by telling the AI which recording to use: camera audio, a wireless mic, a shotgun mic, a dynamic mic, or a separate audio file. If there are several tracks, have it find the main voice, line it up with the picture, and avoid playing two copies together.

![Clean the specific noise without damaging the natural voice.](assets/lesson-06.jpg)

Then describe what you hear. A hiss, low rumble, room echo and uneven volume need different fixes. EQ adjusts the balance of sound frequencies, such as reducing a rumble; compression reduces large volume differences. The [audio guide](../../skills/edit/references/core/AUDIO%20GUIDE.md) helps your AI choose a correction for your recording.

Listen to a short section before and after processing at similar volume. That makes it easier to hear whether the voice improved, since a louder version can seem better just because it's louder. Headphones help reveal small noises and changes in your voice. An ordinary speaker should also make the improvement easy to hear.

For my final audio pass, I use [Auphonic](https://auphonic.com/) with presets named for my microphones. It's optional: you can try it with a free allowance, while additional use costs money. Check [the current plan and its limits](https://auphonic.com/pricing) before uploading.

Do that final pass **after the cuts, color, captions and graphics are finished**, and process the final export once. Keep the version from before Auphonic so a later revision or short can start from audio that hasn't already been enhanced.

If your video demonstrates an audio problem, mark the exact untreated and processed sections in the edit plan before the final pass. Preserve their deliberate differences, including quiet or muted examples, and keep crossfades outside them. Process other narration separately if needed, then check those sections in the finished file. If only captions or framing change after you approve the sound, reuse that audio instead of enhancing it again.
