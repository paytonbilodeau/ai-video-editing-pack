# Keep the face and the point in frame

[CapCut Auto Reframe](https://www.capcut.com/tools/auto-reframe) ·
[CapCut setup](../../../editors/references/EDITOR%20PLAYBOOK%20CAPCUT.md) ·
[Make a short from a longer video](../../../shorts/SKILL.md)

Reframing changes which part of a shot the viewer sees. Start with the editor's
automatic reframing when it is available, then watch the result. A face that
stays visible is useful, but so is the button, product or action being explained.

## Start with the editor

In CapCut Desktop, select the clip and look for **Video → Auto reframe**. Choose
the intended shape, such as 9:16 for a vertical short. The official guide also
describes stabilization and camera-moving-speed controls. Check the controls in
your installed version before following a tutorial. CapCut says Auto Reframe
may require Pro; account, region and version availability can vary.

Let the AI use the supported connection or visible controls. If it cannot
operate one of them, it should give you that exact manual step and continue
afterward. Installing the pack does not add controls to the editor.

## Follow a moving face smoothly

Automatic reframing is a first attempt. Watch the whole shot: does it follow
the intended person, leave enough room around their head and move calmly?

If it follows the wrong person or moves too much, use the editor's supported
subject selection, tracking or position keyframes. A keyframe saves a setting
at a particular moment; the editor moves between those settings. Keep only the
keyframes needed to follow meaningful movement. Following every small head
motion makes the picture wobble. Adjust the movement's easing or smoothing
where available, and check that it does not drift after the person stops.

Use this brief:

> Reframe this clip for a vertical short. Start with the editor's native
> automatic reframing if available. Follow the intended speaker smoothly,
> keeping a stable composition and enough room around their face. Correct any
> tracking loss or switch to another person. Preserve the important demonstration
> as well as the face. Keep the original timeline and make this a separate
> editable version. Watch the full result with captions and graphics visible.

The included standalone waterfall crop tool uses **one fixed crop**. It does
not detect faces or animate the crop. Use the editor route above when the
framing needs to move. This guide explains existing editor capabilities; it
does not add a tracking engine to that command-line tool.

## Leave room for the platform's controls

Look at the current Reels, TikTok or Shorts preview. Account details, captions
and buttons can cover the **top, right and bottom** of the picture. The clear
space left for essential content is often called the safe area. It varies by
platform and placement, so one fixed rectangle is not a universal guarantee.

Keep the main composition centered. If important material overlaps the
interface, scale it back, move a label or change the layout. Do not push the
entire picture left by default. A face above a screen recording may explain a
tutorial better than a tight crop of the face alone.

Watch the actual vertical export at phone size and normal speed. Check the
whole clip, including turns, entrances and transitions. Confirm that faces,
labels and the action remain visible and have time to make sense.
